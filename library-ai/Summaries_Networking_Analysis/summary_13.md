# Summary of Lecture on Log Log Plots and Degree Distribution

## LOG LOG PLOT
- A method to visualize data by taking the logarithm of values on both axes.
- **Definition**: A graph where both the x-axis and y-axis have been logarithmically scaled, enabling clearer visualization of data with a wide range of values.

### Key Characteristics
* Distances between points are equidistant when plotted logarithmically:
  * Distance from 1 to 10 = Distance from 10 to 100 = Distance from 100 to 1,000.
  * Similar spacing applies to smaller values.

## POWER LAW DEGREE DISTRIBUTION
- A common type of distribution seen in network data, indicating a few nodes have very high connections (degree).
- **Definition**: A principle stating that in many real-world networks, the frequency of a node's connections follows a power-law pattern, where a small number of nodes (hubs) have many connections.

### Observations
* A log-log plot can reveal a linear relationship in data that follows a power-law distribution.
* High-degree nodes, though few in number, are prominent in real-world networks like the Internet and social media.
