# Lecture Summary on Network Types and Applications

## **1. Graph Types**
### **1.1 Multi Graphs**
- **Definition**: Graphs that can have multiple edges connecting the same pair of nodes and can also include self-loops (edges that connect nodes to themselves).
- **Example Applications**: Mobile phone networks.

## **2. Directed Networks**
- **Definition**: Networks where connections have a direction; for example, one node points to another but not vice versa.
- **Key Consideration**: Importance is derived from how many nodes point to a particular node, affecting its overall significance.
- **Example**: Websites that link to other websites.

### **2.1 Bidirectional Connections**
- **Definition**: Connections where two nodes point to each other.
- **Example**: Mutual links between two websites.

## **3. Weights on Edges**
- **Definition**: Numerical values assigned to edges representing certain attributes, depending on the application.
- **Example Applications**:
  - **Transportation Networks**: Weights could indicate travel time or passenger capacity.
  - **Airplane Networks**: Weights could indicate the number of people traveling between destinations.

## **4. Bipartite Networks**
- **Definition**: Networks that contain two distinct types of nodes, where connections (edges) can only exist between the two types and not within one type.
- **Example**: 
  - **Amazon Recommendation Systems**: One type represents customers and the other represents products, with edges only between these two categories.
  - **Uber Model**: A person can be a driver in one capacity and a rider in another, allowing for representation in a bipartite manner.

### **4.1 Weight in Bipartite Networks**
- **Definition**: Edges can have weights that represent data such as ratings of a product by a user, indicating the strength of preference.

## **5. Hypergraphs**
- **Definition**: A generalization of graphs where an edge can connect any number of nodes, not just two.
- **Related Concepts**: Similarities to simplicial complexes.

> **Summary**: This lecture covers essential types of networks, including multi graphs, directed networks, weights, bipartite networks, and hypergraphs, with relevant applications.
