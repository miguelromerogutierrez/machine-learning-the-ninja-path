# Lecture Summary on Networks and Data Analysis

## Overview of Large-Scale Data Sets
- Access to a singular, large Excel table of data.
- Rows represent samples (stocks, individuals), and columns represent measured features or covariates.
  
### Example Applications:
* **Stocks:** Price variations over different days.
* **Individuals:** Demographic information and interaction history with a company (e.g., spending behavior).

## Networks and Additional Information
- Networks incorporate links between samples in addition to individual features.
  
### Applications of Network Data:
* Social networks (e.g., friendships, followership).
* Various applications where linked data enhances analysis.

## Definition of a Network
- A **network** (or **graph**) is a collection of nodes (or vertices) connected by links (or edges).
  - Notation: Networks are often denoted as \( G \) where:
    - \( V \) = vertices (nodes)
    - \( E \) = edges (links)

## Historical Context of Network Research
- Network research has gained traction due to advancements in computing technology, allowing the analysis of larger networks (millions of nodes) over previous limitations (10-20 nodes).
