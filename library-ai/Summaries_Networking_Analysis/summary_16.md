# Summary of Social Network Theory Lecture

## Overview of the Small World Experiment
- Discussion of the 1967 experiment on social networks.
- **Key Finding**: On average, only 5.9 steps are required to connect two people within a social network ("six degrees of separation").

### Key Concepts
- **Diameter of a Social Network**: The maximum number of steps needed to connect any two individuals in the network.
- **Sample Size Limitations**: The original experiment had a small sample size of 96 letters, possibly skewing results.

## Facebook as a Case Study
- Opportunity to analyze the diameter of the Facebook network using real data.
- Importance of focusing on the largest connected component of the network.

### Steps in Social Connectivity
- **Hop**: The number of edges or intermediary contacts one has to go through to connect to another individual.
  
#### Percentages of Reachable People
- Explanation of reachability in the network:
  * 0 Steps: Cannot reach anyone.
  * 1 Step: Limited reach (e.g., up to 5,000 friends).
  * 2 Steps: Still low reach percentage.
  * 3 Steps: Increasing reach percentage.
  * 6 Steps: Nearly everyone in the network can be reached.

## Comparing Global and Local Networks
- Diameter remains consistent whether focused on a local network (e.g., U.S.) or at a global scale (e.g., worldwide Facebook).
- Despite growth in the network, the fundamental topological structure leads to similar diameter measurements.

## Summary
> The lecture explains how social networks, particularly Facebook, exhibit a consistent diameter, allowing nearly everyone to connect within six steps globally.

- **Facebook Data Analysis**: Encouraged to compute the network's diameter for educational insights.
- **Social Network Graphs**: Illustrative data on reachability and connections within networks.

## One-Sentence Takeaway
> Understanding social networks helps reveal how interconnected we are and demonstrates that reaching others often requires surprisingly few steps.
